Today

One timeline, not seven trackers

Expenses, habits, food, water, steps, workouts, sleep, calendar events and the weather land in one feed, in the order they happened. You open one screen to see your day, not a tab for each thing you track.

The ring at the top is one number for the whole day, across everything you track.

Trackers

Everything you track, in one place

Six trackers share one account, one timeline and one set of targets.

  • Expenses

    Add one in a few taps, or bring one in: share a payment screenshot or a receipt photo, paste the SMS, or upload a CSV or a bank-statement PDF, even a password-protected one. Split a bill with your contacts and see who owes you. Subscriptions post themselves on the day they are due, and a monthly budget keeps count.

  • Habits

    Tick it, count it, time it or walk it, daily or weekly. Streaks forgive one missed day. Step habits complete themselves from your health data, and each habit can have its own reminder.

  • Nutrition

    Log a meal with its calories and macros, or start from a photo and confirm what it found. The day’s totals sit against a calorie target that is suggested from your body details and applied only if you choose it.

  • Water

    One tap adds a glass, counted against a daily target you set.

  • Body

    Height, weight, sex and date of birth come from your phone’s health store where it holds them, or you type them in. From those it works out BMI and how much energy you use.

  • Reminders and targets

    Reminders are scheduled on your phone, daily or on the weekdays you pick. One screen holds your water, calorie and budget targets.

Completed habits, logged water and a month kept within budget earn XP.

Suggestions

It asks before it assumes

Six rules watch for things worth asking about. Each one shows you what it saw, and nothing is added to your day until you confirm it.

They are plain rules written in code, not AI, so the same day always produces the same question.

When a background sync turns one up, it can reach you as a notification: at most two a day, and none overnight.

  • Possible meal

    “Looks like you had lunch around 1:04 PM”

    Shows the food expense and its time, how often you have made a similar purchase, and an arrival at a known place when location is on.

  • Low water

    “You’ve had 600 ml today”

    Shows your own recent daily average. It never measures you against a population norm.

  • Unusual spending

    “You’ve spent more than usual for a Friday”

    Shows today’s total beside your average for that weekday, and how many of them the average rests on.

  • Low activity

    “A quieter day than usual”

    Shows today’s steps beside your recent average.

  • Habit slipping

    “Reading is usually done by now”

    Shows the time you usually finish it, and how many recent days that comes from.

  • Workout matches a habit

    “Mark Run done?”

    Shows the workout, its length, and that it came from your health data.

Synítheia AI

Insights you can check

AI insights are off until you turn them on. When they are on, a hosted AI model is sent totals and averages that the server has already worked out, such as “food spending averaged ₹482 across four Fridays”. It is never sent a merchant, a note or a place.

Every insight has a “Why am I seeing this?” that shows the exact numbers the model was given, and you can mark an insight helpful or not. Most days it has nothing to say, and that is the right answer.

History

A month at a glance

Each day in the calendar is tinted by how it went, in five steps, so a strong week and a thin one look different at a glance. A day with nothing logged stays an outline.

Tap any day to see it as it was. Past days are read-only.

Limits

What it doesn’t do, and why

Capturing payments without you is not something the phones allow, so the app does not pretend to.

  • It does not read your SMS

    iPhone gives apps no way to. On Android, Google Play allows it only for the app you use as your SMS app.

  • It does not read your notifications

    iPhone gives apps no way to, and store policy on Android does not allow it for tracking expenses.

  • It is not connected to UPI or your bank

    UPI has no public interface that an app like this can use.

So you hand it the data instead: share the payment screenshot, paste the SMS or upload the statement. Screenshots and pasted messages are read by an AI model, and a CSV is read by plain code. Whatever is read arrives as a draft, and nothing becomes an expense until you confirm it.

Download

Get Synítheia

Both builds are free and come from the project’s GitHub releases. You sign in with an email address and a password.

Android

Download the .apk

Latest release

For Android 8.0 or newer.

  1. Download the file.
  2. Open it and, when Android asks, allow your browser to install apps.
  3. Open Synítheia and create an account.

It is not on the Play Store. Install it only from this page or from GitHub Releases.

iPhone

Download the unsigned .ipa

Latest release

This file cannot be installed by tapping it.

  1. Download the file on a computer.
  2. Install it on your phone with Sideloadly or AltStore, signing with your Apple ID.
  3. Open Synítheia and create an account.

With a free Apple ID the app has to be re-signed every seven days, and Apple Health does not work.

What has been checked

  • The Android build was installed, opened and updated from 0.1.1 on an Android 14 emulator. Not yet run on a physical phone.
  • Health on Android, through Health Connect, is new in 0.2.0 and not yet verified on a physical phone.
  • Background sync is best-effort and not yet confirmed on a physical phone.
  • The iPhone build is made by the release pipeline and has not been installed on a phone for this version.
